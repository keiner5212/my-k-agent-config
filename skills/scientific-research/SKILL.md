# Scientific Research

## When to use
- Formulate a research question, hypothesis, or study design.
- Assess validity, reproducibility, or limits of published evidence.
- Synthesize evidence without overstating conclusions.

## Hard rules
- Define the question, population or system, outcomes, and scope before searching.
- Treat correlation as non-causal unless design and analysis support causation.
- Prefer systematic reviews for overview and primary studies for exact methods or results.
- Report effect size, uncertainty interval, sample size, and material limitations when available.
- Separate observed results, interpretation, and recommendation.
- Cite every external factual claim with a direct, stable source link.

## Workflow
1. State a falsifiable question. Use PICO for intervention questions where useful.
2. Define inclusion and exclusion criteria before reviewing sources.
3. Search with synonyms across appropriate databases and official repositories.
4. Rank evidence: systematic review or meta-analysis, primary study or official data,
   preprint or conference work, then commentary.
5. Extract design, population, controls, outcomes, effect estimates, and limitations.
6. Compare results by study quality, not source count alone.
7. State consensus, disagreement, evidence gaps, and confidence.

## Checklist
- [ ] Question is specific, feasible, and measurable.
- [ ] Search terms, sources, and date range are recorded.
- [ ] Inclusion and exclusion criteria are explicit.
- [ ] Study design matches claimed conclusion.
- [ ] Confounders, bias, missing data, and replication are considered.
- [ ] Findings include quantitative results when available.
- [ ] Citations support the exact nearby claim.
- [ ] Uncertainty and generalizability limits are stated.

## Anti-patterns
- Using a single study to establish broad consensus.
- Equating statistical significance with practical importance.
- Treating preprints or press releases as peer-reviewed evidence.
- Omitting null results, contrary evidence, or conflicts of interest.
- Claiming causation from observational association.
