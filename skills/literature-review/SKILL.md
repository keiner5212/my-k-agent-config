# Literature Review

## When to use
- Survey prior work for a defined question or technical topic.
- Produce a reproducible review, evidence map, or state-of-the-art summary.
- Identify consensus, disagreement, evidence quality, and open gaps.

## Hard rules
- Define scope, eligibility criteria, and search method before screening.
- Record databases, queries, date range, and screening decisions.
- Prefer primary studies for findings; use reviews to locate and contextualize work.
- Judge evidence by design and relevance, not citation count or venue alone.
- Cite original work for its findings and quote neither abstract nor secondary summary as proof.
- Separate documented gaps from ideas for future work.

## Workflow
1. Write the question, scope, population or system, outcomes, and time range.
2. Define inclusion and exclusion criteria and required evidence types.
3. Search multiple relevant databases using saved query strings and synonyms.
4. Deduplicate, then screen title, abstract, and full text against the criteria.
5. Extract citation, design, sample, intervention or exposure, outcome, result, and limitations.
6. Build a synthesis matrix and group studies by theme, method, or result.
7. Write a thematic synthesis with evidence strength, conflicts, and gaps.

## Checklist
- [ ] Research question and scope are explicit.
- [ ] Search sources, queries, and dates are recorded.
- [ ] Inclusion and exclusion decisions are consistent.
- [ ] Each finding links to an original paper, dataset, or standard.
- [ ] Methods, populations, and outcome definitions are comparable before pooling conclusions.
- [ ] Funding, conflicts, replication, and publication bias are considered.
- [ ] Review distinguishes consensus, mixed evidence, and unknowns.

## Anti-patterns
- Listing papers in publication order without synthesis.
- Selecting only evidence that supports a desired conclusion.
- Using citation counts as a quality score.
- Generalizing across incompatible populations, methods, or outcomes.
- Calling an unsystematic search comprehensive.
